01 / The problem

Serving faster
does not always make the system better.

Serving one queue can push jobs into a more congested, more expensive downstream stage. Good scheduling must account for task structure, run history, and the opportunity cost of resources at the same time.

Objective: minimize time-average holding cost · cost = ∫ hᵀQ(t) dt / T

Structures differ

Tandem, re-entrant, flexible service. Finishing one stage may only start the next.

Conditions change

Arrivals, service capacity, and randomness mean a fixed priority rule does not always work.

Cost depends on where

On the same network, moving the backlog elsewhere can change the cost completely.

03 / Inside the policy

Structure and time,
in one decision.

STGraph treats every queue, resource pool, and connection as an entity. It exchanges information over the topology, distills experience over time, and outputs a service allocation that satisfies the constraints.

Full architecture / STGRAPH K64

Nodes and edges in the diagram are the queueing network the model processes. Click a module or a button above for details; scroll on narrow screens.

Learn from history, not from the answer

Topology and capacity are visible; λ, μ, h are not inputs. Queue lengths, past actions, event times, and cost feedback provide causal evidence.

Space × time, not simple flattening

Graph messages pass within each time step; each entity then uses a causal TCN over time. The history readout is conditioned on the current state.

Shared weights, constrained actions

Service edges and idle candidates are scored, then passed to a fixed allocation LP. Weights are not updated at inference, and the model never switches by task ID.

04 / Learning from experience

Learn from teachers first.
Then correct the states it actually visits.

Offline demonstrations do not cover every state the model will visit. DAgger lets the policy act in the environment and brings the difficulties it actually meets back into training, instead of only refitting the original trajectories.

01

Multi-task offline pretraining

56 topologies, 8,594 training tasks, 155,210 qualified training trajectories. Four teacher types provide task-specific action supervision.

02

Three rounds of DAgger data collection

States visited by the model → corrections from a fixed teacher; trajectories that already match the teacher provide self-imitation of executed actions. Collection is limited to training tasks.

03

Post-training with a fixed architecture

Restart from the initial weights and train on a mix of base, correction, and self-imitation data for 1,800 optimization updates. Weights are fixed at deployment.

04

One policy, evaluated across tasks

A graph-temporal model with about 9.59M parameters uses the same weights on different networks and schedules from the current structure and run history.

05 / Evidence, not just examples

Beyond the good examples,
look at the full results.

The final QFM · DAgger K64 is compared with five classical baselines on the same task sets. Baselines use an oracle setting: any environment parameters they need are given their true values, with no estimation. MaxQ uses only queue lengths and service connections and needs none of these parameters. QFM does not take the true arrival rates, service rates, or holding costs as input; it decides from the visible structure and run feedback.
